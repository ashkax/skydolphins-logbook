6-Month Training Syllabus (Zero to Independent Pilot) Self-Paced Progression Checklist
Overall Training Progress: 0% Completed
Month 1
Ground Handling & Kiting Fundamentals
Mastering the glider on flat terrain before touching mountain launches
▼
Canopy Anatomy & Equipment Care

Learn lines classification (A, B, C, brakes), speed system, harness adjustments, reserve pin check, and proper packing.

Forward Launch Inflation & Running

Building the wall, smooth torso forward drive, keeping glider centered overhead on gentle 5-10 km/h breezes.

Reverse Launch & Active Kiting (15+ Hours)

Turning around in harness, controlling canopy with A-risers and brakes in 12-20 km/h wind without looking up constantly.

Kill Canopy Technique & Strong Wind Safety

Emergency de-powering using C/D risers or rear brake wrap to prevent getting dragged in gusty conditions.

Month 2
Slope Hops & First Solo Mountain Flights
Transition to training hill, radio communication, and high takeoffs
▼
Training Slope Hops (20-50m elevation)

Low glides down grassy slopes to master smooth flare timing, landing upright on your feet, and commitment to the run.

Tandem Instructional Flights with Certified Instructor

Dual flights to experience high altitude sensations, airspeed control, and landing pattern from 400m+.

First High Solo Flights (e.g. Mahdirajeh or Sarket)

First solo launches under radio supervision. Clean launch run, smooth glide in calm morning/afternoon air, safe landing.

Landing Pattern (Box / DBF: Downwind, Base, Final)

Judging wind cone, setting up accurate altitude depletion, and landing into wind within 20m of target.

Month 3
Dynamic Ridge Soaring & Airspace Mastery
Staying aloft in continuous coastal/mountain sea breeze lift
▼
Figure-8 Ridge Turns & Lift Exploration

Turning away from the mountain ridge at all times; maintaining minimum safe airspeed while flying parallel to slope.

Air Traffic & Right-of-Way Rules

Ridge with right shoulder has right of way; passing protocols; distance preservation from other paragliders.

Extended Flight Duration (1+ Hour Continuous Flight)

Sustaining flight in steady 15-18 km/h Caspian onshore wind at sites like Mahdirajeh without touching down prematurely.

Speed System (Speed Bar) Operation

Engaging 30% and 50% speed bar to penetrate stronger headwind layers safely without pitching forward.

Month 4
Active Piloting & Rapid Descent Maneuvers
Pitch dampening, roll stability, and tactical altitude loss
Pitch & Surge Control (Active Piloting)

Catching surges immediately when entering/leaving thermals or gusts to prevent frontal collapses.

Big Ears Maneuver (Ears + Speed Bar)

Pulling outer A-lines to reduce sail area and increase sink rate to 3-4 m/s while maintaining directional steering with weight shift.

Mild Asymmetric Collapse Recovery Training

Simulating 30% asymmetric collapse, counter-weight-shifting to maintain heading, and gently pumping clear.

Top Landing Intro & Touch-and-Go Drills

Learning how to approach the takeoff ridge back at altitude in gentle, smooth conditions.

Month 5
Thermal Soaring & Introduction to Cross-Country (XC)
Catching rising warm air currents and reading cloud bases
Variometer & Flight Instrument Competency

Interpreting audio tones, averaging climb rate, and understanding glide ratios to potential landing zones.

Centering Thermal Cores

Tightening turns on strong beep, flattening bank angle when sink begins; drifting with the wind gradient.

Cross-Country (XC) Flight Plan Preparation

Analyzing terrain, identifying power lines, restricted airspace, and pre-planning safe out-landing fields.

Alpine Flying Experience (e.g. Kelardasht or Damavand Foothills)

Adapting to higher density altitude, stronger mid-day thermal turbulence, and valley wind patterns.

Month 6
SIV Safety Clinic, Certification & Independence
Over-water safety training, formal pilot license test, and autonomous flying
Over-Water SIV Course (Simulation d'Incidents en Vol)

Full stalls, dynamic spin recovery, aggressive spirals, frontal collapses, and reserve toss deployment over lake/sea.

Independent Site & Weather Risk Assessment

Making go/no-go calls completely autonomously based on microclimate, local cloud formations, and wind gradient.

Theory Exam & Aerodynamics Written Test

Passing aviation meteorology, micro-meteorology, airspace regulations, and flight mechanics test.

Final Practical Checkride & License Award (P2/P3 / APPI)

Accurate spot landing, demonstrated active flying in moderate conditions, and solo license issuance.

Mazandaran & North Iran 6-Month Seasonal Strategy Pick the Best 6-Month Block

To finish your 6-month training program with minimal weather cancellations, aligning your training with the northern climatic calendar is critical.

Iranian Month (Solar) Gregorian Period Flyable Days / Mo Dominant Weather & Wind Recommended Training Focus
Farvardin - Ordibehesht Late Mar - May 12 - 16 Days Spring transition. Moderate rain fronts, rising thermals, changing winds. Ground handling, simulator practice, calm morning slope hops.
Khordad - Tir Late May - July 20 - 24 Days Stable onshore sea breezes (12-18 km/h). Consistent afternoon ridge lift. Ridge soaring, high flights, long duration flights at Mahdirajeh.
Mordad - Shahrivar Late July - Sept 22 - 25 Days Peak warm weather. High humidity; strong afternoon thermals in mountains. Active piloting, high altitude soaring in Kelardasht, thermaling.
Mehr - Aban Late Sept - Nov 14 - 18 Days Crisp, clear autumn days. Lower humidity, shorter daylight, occasional fronts. Spot landing precision, SIV clinic over water, license practical tests.
Azar - Dey - Bahman Dec - Feb 4 - 8 Days Heavy rainfall, wet landing pastures, low cloud ceilings, snow on ridges. Ground theory, reserve repack, harness adjustments, indoor simulator.
Optimal 6-Month Training Windows:

Best Choice: Start in April (Ordibehesht) and conclude in October (Mehr). This spans the entire dry, thermic, and sea-breeze season with virtually no long winter interruptions.

Pre-Flight 5-Point Safety Check

Perform this sacred routine on the takeoff launch pad before pulling up the canopy on every single flight.

1. Helmet & Harness Leg/Chest Buckles Helmet chinstrap buckled tight; leg loops double-checked; main chest strap secure with T-lock engaged.
2. Carabiners & Speed System Main carabiners fully closed and locked (no cross-loading). Speed bar lines run cleanly outside risers.
3. Reserve Parachute Container & Handle Deployment handle seated firmly, safety pin in position, container zip closed, bridle attached properly.
4. Canopy Layout, Lines & Risers Canopy open in horseshoe arc, leading edge clear of sticks/stones; A-risers clean on top; brake lines clear of wraps.
5. Wind Direction & Airspace Clearance Wind speed within envelope; wind direct onto takeoff; no other pilots in landing zone or launch corridor. Clear for launch!
